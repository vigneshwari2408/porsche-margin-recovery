01

01The question

Is Porsche's 2026 margin recovery actually improving underneath the reported numbers?

Reported margin (L0)

5.5%to7.8%

Up 2.28 points, H1 2025 to H1 2026. The reported headline.

Underlying margin (L1)

9.4%to8.4%

Down 0.99 points once Porsche's own disclosed realignment and battery items are removed.

After two difficult half-years, the headline recovered. But a recovering headline can hide three things: smaller one-off charges rather than a better business, accounting effects from R&D capitalisation or depreciation, and a richer price and mix compensating for fewer cars sold.

This case study tests each of them, using only numbers Porsche has published, and then asks what Porsche's own FY2026 guidance requires from the second half.

02

02Executive overview

The headline rose because the one‑offs shrank.

The Executive Overview page of the Power BI dashboard puts reported and underlying profit side by side. L0 is Porsche's reported EBIT. L1 adds back the disclosed strategic realignment and battery items, and is the headline underlying KPI. L2 also adds back US import tariffs.

Until 2025 the three lines are identical, because no one-offs were disclosed. From H1 2025 they separate, and in H1 2026 the reported line rises while both adjusted lines are below the same half a year earlier.

03EBIT recovery

Without the one‑off relief, EBIT would have fallen.

The year-on-year bridge explains the change in reported EBIT against the same half of the prior year. Each step is either a disclosed adjustment, an accounting effect, the Financial Services segment, or the residual: the part published data cannot split into price, mix, volume, cost and FX.

Lower one-offs contributed +600 € million, which is 176% of the whole reported gain. Everything else, taken together, moved EBIT down.

04R&D and D&A

Accounting is a headwind, not a hidden tailwind.

A company can lift EBIT by capitalising more of its development spend, or by booking less depreciation. The test here is whether that happened. It did not: the share of R&D capitalised has fallen in every first half since 2023, and amortisation of what was capitalised earlier now pushes the P&L charge above the R&D costs themselves.

What about L3, the hybrid sensitivity?

L3 adds clean automotive D&A back to L1 and deducts capitalised development costs, a figure in which all R&D is expensed as incurred. It mixes group EBIT with automotive-only items, so it is a supplementary sensitivity, never a KPI. In H1 2026 it is 2,459 € million (14.3% of revenue), or 2,520 if the €61 million of impairments are not inside D&A (assumption A-04). It is shown only to size the non-cash wedge, and is never compared with guidance.

05Commercial drivers

Value over volume works, but only partly.

120,508vehicles sold, −10.8%
€125.8kaverage selling price, +5.3%

Porsche sold 10.8% fewer vehicles, while the average selling price rose 5.3% to €125.8k. The higher price recovered 44% of the revenue lost to volume. The ASP effect is price plus model mix, options and FX, not pure pricing.

Scenario analysis

Arithmetic only — not forecasts — no probabilities

Porsche guides FY2026 as ranges. Each range is taken at its low, mid or high end and the ends are combined, 27 ways for the group and 9 for the automotive segment. For each combination, the H2 result is what the full year implies once H1 2026 actuals are deducted. "All midpoints" is not a most-likely case.
